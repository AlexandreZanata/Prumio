# Product vision

## The question that guides every decision

> **How can a person spend less time managing their organization and more time living their life?**

Prumio (from *prumo*, the plumb line: balance and direction) is a personal organizer whose job is
to **remember things for you**, so they stop occupying your head. It must never become one more
thing you have to administer.

It is not another Notion, Todoist or Google Calendar. It is a calm, private assistant for the
small and large commitments of everyday life — offline, open source, free.

## Who it is for

People with ordinary, busy lives: bills, appointments, errands, family logistics, bureaucracy,
repairs, requests that drag on for weeks. Not project managers; not teams. One person, one phone.

## Three kinds of commitments

| Kind | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | What do I need to do? | Pay the internet bill · Buy groceries · Send the document |
| **Appointment** | Where do I need to be? | Doctor Thursday 16:00 · Meeting · Visit |
| **Matter** | What do I need to resolve in my life? | Sort out the vehicle documents · Organize finances · Follow up on a request |

**Matters are Prumio's identity.** A matter is not a single task: it is a subject that stays
open, moves through *Open → Waiting → Resolved*, has a *next action* ("Call the dispatcher
tomorrow at 9") and a short *history* ("Request sent — Nov 10 · Waiting for reply — Nov 11").
When you mark it as waiting, Prumio asks when to check again, and reminds you. You no longer have
to keep the unfinished thing in your head.

## Capture first, organize later

Thoughts arrive at the worst moments. Capturing must take seconds: open, type "change the
motorcycle oil", save. No project, priority or category required. It goes to the **Inbox**; later,
it becomes a task, an appointment or a matter — or is discarded.

```
Something comes to mind → written down in seconds → (later) task · appointment · matter
```

## Principles

1. **Zero effort to capture.** A title is enough to save anything (BR-GEN-001). Dates, times,
   recurrence, labels: optional.
2. **Matters as a first-class concept.** Next action, history, waiting state, follow-up.
3. **Attention without excess.** Relevant reminders; no streaks, scores, rankings, badges or
   artificial pressure (BR-REVIEW-002). Calm language.
4. **Nothing disappears by being ignored.** An ignored notification changes nothing; the task stays
   open until completed, rescheduled or dismissed (BR-GEN-006).
5. **Private by design.** No network permission, no account, no telemetry, no ads (BR-PRIV-*).
   The user owns the data and its backups.
6. **Predictable and transparent.** Local rules anyone can read (business-rules.md). No AI, no remote
   processing, no "smart" behavior the user cannot explain.
7. **Reliable, honestly.** Reminders as reliable as the OS allows, and clear messages when the
   device prevents it (exact alarms, battery savers).

## Differentiation

Good open-source task managers exist (Tasks.org, Super Productivity). Prumio does not compete on
feature count. It differs by three product decisions: effortless capture, matters as a central
concept, and attention without excess.

## Scope

**In scope until Android v1.0:** inbox and capture (button, widget, share, shortcut, voice),
tasks, agenda, matters, Today, reminders and notifications (snooze, quiet hours, morning summary,
follow-ups), recurrence, search, weekly life review, labels, backup/restore (plain and encrypted),
calendar export/import (.ics), English and Brazilian Portuguese, accessibility.

**Out of scope until explicitly requested by the owner:** iOS, desktop and web clients; sync between
devices; sharing or collaboration; attachments; any server or account; AI features.

## The weekly life review

Once a week, Prumio discreetly presents what is stalled, what has no next action and what is
coming — with plain local rules, no AI. It turns a calendar-with-tasks into a personal system to
**capture, remember, follow up and resolve**.

## How the product evolves

The owner uses Prumio daily for real tasks, appointments, bureaucracy and small matters. Friction
found in that use drives the next changes — before any new feature.
